HOW TO READ THE CV TEXT:
The CV text was extracted from a PDF file. Fonts, colors, columns
and the exact layout are not available, so never comment on them.
- A line break in the text often only marks where a line wrapped in
  the PDF, so do not judge the length of a sentence by its line
  breaks.
- Bullet symbols are often lost. Separate points may be joined on
  one line or split across lines, often with no full stop between
  them, and a heading may run onto the same line as the text after
  it. A capitalized verb in the middle of a line, with no joining
  word such as "and" before it, usually marks the start of a new
  point. These effects come from the extraction, not from the CV, so
  do not report them.
- Links can be hidden behind words in the PDF. If the text shows a
  link label such as LinkedIn, GitHub or Portfolio without an
  address, do not say the link is missing. Suggest also writing the
  full address as visible text (the link can stay clickable),
  because some ATS read only the text.
- Headings may appear in capital letters. Do not report the letter
  case of a heading.

TASK:
Evaluate the CV in exactly these five areas. Before you answer, go
through every check listed under every area, in order, against the
whole CV. Then report only the checks that find a real problem.

1. cvStructure
   a. Expected sections: whether the CV has the sections expected for
      a Computing or IT student or graduate: contact information,
      Education, Skills, Projects or Experience, and Certifications
      when the person lists any certificates. A section counts as
      present when its content is there under any heading. A badly
      named heading is a sectionOrganization issue, not a missing
      section.
   b. Contact details, checked together: full name, an email address
      written in the standard name@domain form, a phone number, and a
      city or country. For Computing and IT roles, a LinkedIn profile
      or a GitHub or portfolio link is also expected. Report every
      missing or malformed contact detail together as one issue, and
      name each one.
   c. Section order: whether the sections appear in a logical order
      for a student or fresh graduate. When you suggest a new order,
      list every section of the CV in the full new order.

2. sectionOrganization
   a. Headings: whether every section heading is a standard name that
      an ATS recognizes, such as Summary, Education, Experience, Work
      Experience, Projects, Skills, Technical Skills, Certifications,
      Activities, Volunteering, Awards or Languages. Report a heading
      that is misspelled, or that describes the section in personal
      or creative words instead of a standard name, and give the
      standard name to use. Common clear variants, such as
      Professional Experience, Academic Projects, Relevant
      Coursework, Training, Research, Publications, Leadership,
      Honors, Achievements, Interests or Extracurricular Activities,
      are standard and must not be reported.
   b. Placement: whether each item sits in the section where an ATS
      and a recruiter would look for it. For example, a project
      written inside Education or inside a job entry, a job listed
      under Projects, or a certificate listed under Education. Name
      the item and the section it belongs in.
   c. Order inside sections: whether the entries of each section are
      in reverse chronological order (most recent first). When you
      suggest a reorder, list every entry of that section in the full
      correct order, not only the entry that moves.

3. keywordUsage
   a. Tool and technology names: whether every programming language,
      library, framework, tool and platform the CV mentions is written
      in its official form (the spelling, spacing, punctuation and
      capitalization its makers use), and whether the same name is
      written the same way everywhere. Name each name that is not in
      its official form and give the official form.
   b. Standard terms: whether work that the CV describes in plain,
      everyday words is also named with the standard technical term
      that an ATS or a recruiter would search for. Suggest a term only
      when the description clearly shows that technique. Never add a
      specific algorithm, model architecture, library or number that
      the CV does not state. If a detail is likely but not stated,
      write it as a condition, such as "if you used a specific
      algorithm, name it".
   c. Relevant keywords: whether skills and tools that the CV already
      shows in its projects or experience, and that matter for the
      target career goal, are also listed where an ATS looks for
      them, such as the Skills section or the Summary.
   Never report a skill, tool, platform, field or certification that
   the CV does not mention at all. A missing skill is a skill gap,
   which Masra covers in a separate feature. It is not a CV problem.

4. readability
   a. Length: whether any sentence is very long (roughly more than 40
      words that read as one sentence, with its parts joined by
      commas or by words such as "and", "so" or "which"), or any
      entry of several sentences is written as one continuous story
      instead of separate short points. Points that the extraction
      has joined on one line are not one long sentence, even without
      full stops between them, and a short entry of one or two
      sentences is fine. Name the entry and suggest splitting it into
      short points.
   b. Wording: whether each point starts with a strong action verb,
      or instead lists duties, uses vague words, or is written as a
      first-person story.
   c. Results: whether achievements give a specific result where one
      is possible. Ask the user to add their own figure, using a
      placeholder such as [number] or [percentage]. Never invent one.

5. contentFormatting
   a. Dates: whether dates use one consistent format across the CV,
      and whether any date contradicts other text in the CV or
      today's date.
   b. Consistent writing: whether the same kind of detail is written
      the same way in every entry (for example abbreviations, or how
      degrees, job titles and places are written), and whether the
      text has spelling mistakes that no other area has reported.
      Never report bullet symbols, line breaks, spacing or the letter
      case of headings, because the extracted text cannot show them
      reliably. Contact details are checked under cvStructure, not
      here.
